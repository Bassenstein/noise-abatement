HRSG Casing & Duct
Noise Minimization Guide

Design and operational controls to keep Heat Recovery Steam Generator casing breakout and duct-path noise below the City of Cleveland's fenceline threshold — protecting community relations and permit standing at all operating conditions.

Governing Ordinance COC §605.10 + Ch. 683
Day Limit (7 am – 10 pm) 65 dBA @ property line
Night Limit (10 pm – 7 am) 55 dBA @ property line
Measurement Standard A-weighted dBA, Leq
Code Authority §605.10 Unnecessary Noise — prohibits sound of character, intensity, or duration detrimental to health or peace of neighboring inhabitants
Standard Residential Fenceline 65 / 55 dBA day / night. Ohio practice; enforced at receiving property line, A-weighted, continuous equivalent level (Leq)
Industrial Zone Adjacent 70 / 65 dBA day / night where adjacent land is commercial or heavy industrial — confirm with Dept. of Building & Housing on permit
⚠ Design-Phase Priority: Retrofitting noise mitigation on an operating HRSG after commissioning can cost $1M – $5M+ in cladding, labor, and downtime. Cleveland municipal code penalizes each day of continuing violation as a separate offense. Build the budget for noise control into the HRSG specification — not the contingency line.

Casing & Duct Mitigation Techniques
01
Casing — Structural Acoustic
Increased Casing Plate Thickness + External Stiffeners
−8 to −15 dB
▾

The HRSG casing is the primary radiating surface for solid-borne noise. Sound energy from high-velocity exhaust gas couples into the casing panels and re-radiates as airborne noise. Specifying heavier-gauge outer skin — typically 10–12 gauge (3.4–2.8 mm) vs. the standard 14-gauge minimum — directly increases the mass-law transmission loss of the panel.

External stiffeners welded at reduced spacing (typically every 24–36 in. instead of 48 in.) stiffen the panel coincidence frequency above the troublesome 63–250 Hz octave bands, raising the effective STC of the wall assembly.

Plate gauge: 10–12 ga (3.0–3.4 mm)
Stiffener pitch: ≤ 30 in. o.c.
IL estimate: 8–15 dB
Cost tier: Low — spec at procurement

Ensure stiffener-to-casing welds are continuous, not intermittent — cracked welds at stiffener terminations are a primary noise leakage path and a structural failure precursor noted in long-term HRSG inspection records.

02
Casing — Thermal & Acoustic
Acoustic Cladding System (Double-Wall Assembly)
−10 to −20 dB
▾

A double-wall acoustic cladding assembly applied over the HRSG outer casing is the primary control for casing breakout noise where the base casing spec alone cannot meet the budget. The assembly typically consists of: (1) a high-density mineral wool or ceramic fiber batt acoustic fill, (2) a decoupled outer metal jacket (galvanized or aluminized steel), and (3) a resilient isolation layer to prevent solid-borne vibration bridging between the casing and the jacket.

The air gap and mass-spring-mass effect of the double-wall system provides substantially higher transmission loss than a single-wall cladding approach — critical for low-frequency tonal content below 250 Hz that single-wall assemblies fail to attenuate.

Fill: 4–6 in. mineral wool, ≥ 6 pcf
Outer jacket: 18-ga galv. steel
Air gap: 1–2 in. minimum
IL estimate: 10–20 dB

For HRSGs exceeding 80 ft in height, the cost of adding a full acoustic shroud post-construction can run into the millions due to elevated labor, scaffolding, and the requirement to shut down the plant. Specify this at the OEM level before fabrication.

03
Duct Path — In-Duct Attenuation
Horizontal Splitter Silencers in HRSG Gas Path
−15 to −25 dB
▾

Horizontal splitter silencers installed within the HRSG inlet duct or within the HRSG itself are the single most effective acoustic intervention for exhaust-path noise. The silencers are absorption-type units — a perforated inner skin filled with mineral wool or ceramic fiber packaged in high-temperature stainless steel housings — that attenuate broadband noise traveling down the exhaust stream before it radiates from the stack or couples into casing panels.

The finned tube bundles in a well-designed HRSG already provide 10–15 dB of passive attenuation to the exhaust stream. Adding dedicated silencer bays upstream of the tube banks (in the inlet duct) or between module sections provides additive reduction on top of this baseline.

IL: 15–25 dB insertion loss
Temp rating: to 1,200°F (650°C)
Material: 304/316 SS perforated + mineral wool
Pressure drop: ≤ 0.35 in. w.c. target

Silencer pressure drop must be managed carefully — excess back-pressure degrades gas turbine output and heat rate. Request silencer performance curves with both IL and Δp vs. mass flow from the vendor and confirm compatibility with the GT's exhaust pressure tolerance at max load.

04
Duct Path — Liner System
Inlet Duct Acoustic Liner & Stud Integrity Program
−5 to −12 dB
▾

The GT-to-HRSG transition duct is an unimpeded noise conduit if left unlined. A properly installed acoustic liner system — typically a metallic hot face (perforated stainless liner plate), acoustic infill (high-temperature ceramic fiber blanket), and an attachment stud array — provides both thermal protection for the duct skin and broadband absorption of turbine exhaust noise within the duct bore before it enters the HRSG body.

Liner integrity is critical. Inspection records consistently show that failed studs — which allow liner plates to shift and expose insulation to the flow — not only eliminate the acoustic benefit but create SCR catalyst blinding from liberated ceramic fiber. Specify a stud inspection and torque-verification protocol at each annual borescope outage.

Hot face: Perf. 316 SS, ≥ 25% open area
Fill: 2–4 in. ceramic fiber, 8 pcf min
Stud pitch: ≤ 12 in. o.c.
IL: 5–12 dB broadband

Gap sealing between liner panels is acoustically important — even small gaps allow the acoustic energy to bypass the liner and couple directly into the duct shell, short-circuiting the attenuation.

05
Casing — Vibration Isolation
Structural Vibration Decoupling & Anti-Fatigue Mounts
−4 to −10 dB
▾

Solid-borne vibration travels from the inlet duct through attached steel structure and into the HRSG casing, bypassing any airborne acoustic treatments. Vibration isolation mounts at the GT exhaust flange, duct support saddles, and HRSG foundation pads break this solid path and dramatically reduce the structural excitation of the casing radiating surfaces.

Neoprene or steel-spring isolation elements rated for the operating temperature range should be specified at every structural hard point. For the high-temperature regions of the inlet duct (>400°F), high-temp elastomeric or metallic spring isolators with stable stiffness across the operating temperature range are required.

Isolator type: Neoprene pad / metal spring
Isolation efficiency: ≥ 90% target (10 dB)
Natural freq.: < 8 Hz insertion freq.
Temp range: to 300°F exterior surface
06
Stack — Terminal Attenuation
Stack-Top Vertical Silencer & Acoustic Stack Lining
−10 to −30 dB
▾

The exhaust stack top is often the dominant noise source at fenceline when low-frequency content is present, because low-frequency sound diffracts around barriers and propagates efficiently at ground level. Vertical silencers installed in the stack — either at the base (before the stack body) or integrated into the stack structure — address the stack-top emission path directly.

Stack lining with acoustically absorptive material along the interior wall provides distributed attenuation as sound propagates up the stack. The lining must be rated for the full exhaust temperature range and protected with a perforated metallic hot face to prevent erosion by the flow stream. Where the stack is the primary noise driver, purpose-designed acoustic stacks with integral silencer modules can achieve 20–30 dB insertion loss on the exhaust path.

Silencer IL: 10–30 dB (frequency-dependent)
Stack lining: 3–4 in. ceramic fiber + perf SS
Min stack diam. retained: per GT backpressure spec
Retrofit cost: $300K–$1M+ per stack

Tonal content at specific frequencies (often from combustion dynamics or rotating machinery) may require tuned reactive silencer elements in addition to absorption — request a 1/3-octave band noise spectrum from the GT OEM and match the silencer design to the specific frequency signature.

07
Site — External Barrier
Fenceline Acoustic Barrier Wall
−5 to −15 dB
▾

A solid perimeter barrier wall between the HRSG equipment pad and the fenceline provides insertion loss for mid-to-high-frequency noise paths. Barriers work most effectively when: (1) the source height is below the barrier top, (2) the barrier extends well past the sides of the equipment, and (3) the barrier surface has absorptive treatment to prevent reflection back to the community on the opposite side.

Barrier effectiveness is frequency-dependent — high-frequency noise is well attenuated, but low-frequency content below 125 Hz diffracts readily over barriers and sees minimal insertion loss. This makes barriers a secondary, not primary, control for gas-turbine exhaust noise, which has significant low-frequency energy. Design barriers as a supplement to source and path controls, not a substitute.

Barrier height: ≥ source height + 3 ft min
STC rating: ≥ STC-30 recommended
IL @ 1 kHz: 10–15 dB
IL @ 63 Hz: 1–3 dB (low-freq limited)

Masonry block or concrete panels with an absorptive face (mineral wool-clad metal facing) perform best. All-metal barriers can reflect noise toward other receptors. Consult an acoustic consultant for barrier siting when HRSG height exceeds 40 ft.

08
Tube Bundle — Vibration Control
Tube Tie Inspection, Replacement & Anti-Vibration Baffles
−3 to −8 dB
▾

Vortex-induced vibration of finned tube bundles ("rattling") is a persistent secondary noise source in operating HRSGs. Failed tube-tie components — fin tab welds in particular — allow tubes to vibrate at their natural frequency, producing tonal noise that couples through the casing. Because fin tabs are harder than tube material, failed ties also produce progressive tube wear that worsens over time.

Specify a tube-tie inspection at every outage, with particular attention to lower-elevation bundles (easiest access). Add center baffles to any inlet-duct superheater panel missing them — missing center baffles are a confirmed precursor to rattling in HRSG inspection literature.

Inspection interval: Annual borescope
Repair standard: Replace failed ties; re-torque all
Anti-vibration baffles: Every tube row @ inlet

Fenceline Monitoring Protocol — Cleveland Compliance
1
Pre-Construction Acoustic Study
Commission a licensed acoustical consultant to establish existing ambient baseline dBA at all fenceline receptor points. Measure over a minimum 72-hour period including night-time hours to establish the Leq ambient below which any HRSG contribution must remain. Document in octave bands (31.5 Hz – 8 kHz).
2
Model-Based Noise Prediction
Use ISO 9613-2 or CONCAUL propagation modeling software to predict fenceline levels at full-load HRSG operation. Include all source contributions (casing breakout, stack, piping) as point and line sources. Adjust mitigation spec until the model predicts ≥ 5 dB margin below the 65 dBA day / 55 dBA night Cleveland threshold.
3
Commissioning Verification Survey
Conduct A-weighted Leq measurements at all fenceline receptor points during HRSG first-fire and ramp to full load. Use a Type 1 or Type 2 sound level meter per ANSI S1.4 at 1.2 m height, minimum 15-minute averaging periods. Confirm measured levels are ≤ 63 dBA (day) to maintain a 2 dB operational margin against the code limit.
4
Continuous or Periodic Monitoring Station
Install a permanent or semi-permanent Class 2 noise monitoring station at the fenceline receptor closest to the HRSG and any adjacent residential property. Configure the logger to flag exceedances above 62 dBA (day) / 52 dBA (night) for immediate operational review — these trip points provide a 3 dB alert margin before code violation.
5
Annual Compliance Survey & O&M Trigger
Conduct a full fenceline survey annually and after any HRSG major outage. Correlate any level increases with maintenance findings (cladding damage, failed studs, casing cracks) and repair immediately. Document all surveys; Cleveland §605.10 enforcement is complaint-driven but consecutive-day violations accrue separately.
Pre-Permit Compliance Checklist
Note: The City of Cleveland does not publish a specific industrial dBA fenceline limit as a separate table — compliance is evaluated under the §605.10 "unreasonably loud, disturbing" standard and the broadly enforced Ohio practice of 65/55 dBA (day/night) at the receiving property line. Confirm the applicable limit with the Dept. of Building & Housing during the permit pre-application meeting. The checklist below targets that standard.
  • Acoustic consultant engaged prior to HRSG specification; pre-construction ambient survey complete
  • HRSG casing spec requires ≥ 10-gauge outer plate on all four walls and roof panel
  • External stiffeners specified at ≤ 30 in. o.c. with continuous fillet welds
  • Acoustic double-wall cladding system specified on all HRSG external surfaces (4–6 in. MW / decoupled outer jacket)
  • Horizontal splitter silencer bay included in HRSG scope — vendor to provide IL curves + Δp curves at full load
  • Stack vertical silencer specified; stack design confirmed below GT maximum back-pressure
  • Inlet duct acoustic liner system (perf. SS hot face + ceramic fiber fill) in OEM scope
  • Vibration isolation mounts specified at GT exhaust flange, duct support points, and HRSG foundation
  • Noise propagation model (ISO 9613-2) completed; fenceline predicted levels ≤ 60 dBA day / 50 dBA night (≥ 5 dB margin)
  • Commissioning measurement plan drafted; post-startup fenceline survey required before full commercial operation
  • Fenceline monitoring station sited and configured with 62/52 dBA alert thresholds
  • Annual noise compliance survey schedule documented in O&M manual; HRSG casing integrity included in outage inspection scope
  • Night-time operation confirmed compliant at 55 dBA limit — duct-burner operation at reduced load if needed
