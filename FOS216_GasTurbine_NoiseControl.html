01 · Regulatory Basis

Cleveland Noise Code & Applicable Thresholds

Cleveland's codified noise standard is primarily a nuisance-based framework under Chapter 605.10 of the Codified Ordinances. Section 605.10(b)(5) specifically prohibits "the unreasonable discharge into the open air of the exhaust of any steam engine, stationary internal combustion engine, motor vehicle or motor boat engine." Gas turbines operating on the campus fall directly within scope of this provision.

Ohio has no statewide dBA limit for industrial stationary sources — regulation is fully delegated to municipalities. Cleveland's ordinance does not specify explicit octave-band limits, but enforcement practice and permit conditions applied by the Department of Building & Housing and Ohio EPA PTIO (Permit-to-Install and Operate) processes align with the statewide de facto benchmarks applied by other industrial-zone authorities in Northeast Ohio. For hyperscale facility permitting and community compliance planning, The FixOurStreets216 Project applies the following binding design targets:

02 · Source Identification

Turbine–Stack Noise Sources & Baseline Levels

A gas turbine power package emits noise through several distinct pathways. Understanding which pathway dominates at which frequency is the foundation of any effective treatment strategy. The Kawasaki L30A and GPB17MMX packages ship from the factory with acoustic enclosures rated at 85 dBA at 1 m to the side of the enclosure — the raw exhaust and intake discharge, not enclosure shell radiation, typically govern fenceline performance at distance.

03 · Engineering Controls

Four-Layer Treatment Strategy

Achieving ≤ 60 dBA at the fenceline from a gas turbine operating at 110+ dBA at the exhaust exit requires a minimum of ~50 dBA combined attenuation through geometric spreading, silencing, and secondary barriers. No single measure achieves this — the following four layers must be stacked in sequence.

Layer 1 · Primary Path Control

Inline Exhaust & Inlet Silencers

The highest-ROI intervention. A purpose-engineered absorptive/reactive hybrid silencer inserted in the exhaust duct immediately downstream of the turbine exit (before the HRSG inlet or bypass stack) delivers 20–35 dBA of broadband attenuation. For the L30A's exhaust temperature (~500 °C), silencers must use high-density mineral wool (min. 200 kg/m³), stainless liner, and perforated facing rated for continuous high-temperature service.

An inlet air silencer in the filter house/intake plenum targets the 500–4000 Hz band. Standard 2-stage filtration housings from Kawasaki include a basic attenuating splitter — upgrade to a custom deep-insertion silencer for campus installations.

−25 to −35 dBA exhaust −15 to −20 dBA inlet
Layer 2 · HRSG & Duct Treatment

Casing Lagging & Vent Silencers

HRSG module casing and interconnecting exhaust ductwork radiates aerodynamic and structure-borne noise across the 125–1000 Hz band. Acoustic lagging (50–75 mm mineral wool blanket + reinforced foil or steel jacket) achieves −8 to −12 dBA of shell-radiated noise. All pressure-relief valves and blowdown vents must be fitted with purpose-designed vent silencers — these event sources can spike to 120 dBA and are explicitly targeted by §605.10(b)(5). Use reactive-type vent silencers (Solberg or Maxim equivalent) rated for the vent orifice Cv.

The HRSG stack itself should incorporate internally-baffled acoustical panels at the top section where structural geometry allows — this provides −5 to −8 dBA of additional vertical stack exit attenuation.

−8 to −12 dBA lagging Vent silencers mandatory
Layer 3 · Secondary Barriers

Acoustic Wall / Berm System

When residences or sensitive receptors exist within 300 m of the plant boundary, an acoustic barrier wall (masonry, precast concrete, or mass-loaded modular panel) placed between the turbine package and the fenceline provides −5 to −15 dBA depending on height and geometry relative to the sound path. Barrier effectiveness is frequency-dependent: barriers perform best above 250 Hz and offer limited benefit for the 63–125 Hz octave bands that dominate turbine exhaust.

Earth berms with planted facing provide equivalent IL with the added benefit of absorbing low-frequency energy more effectively than rigid reflective walls. A 3–4 m berm with sloped profile and dense vegetation topping is recommended where site geometry permits.

−5 to −15 dBA (geometry-dependent) LF limited — supplement with silencer
Layer 4 · Geometric & Operational

Siting, Stack Height & Load Management

Stack height directly affects ground-level fenceline levels — the inverse square law delivers approximately −6 dB per doubling of distance. A 30 m stack discharging vertically at 78 dBA will arrive at a 150 m fenceline at approximately 56 dBA (before propagation adjustments). Orienting exhaust stacks to discharge away from sensitive receptors and maximizing horizontal setback are zero-cost interventions.

For nighttime compliance (55 dBA limit), turbines operating at reduced load (turn-down to 50–70% output) reduce source levels by −3 to −6 dBA depending on the unit. The Kawasaki L30A DLE combustor is optimized for part-load stability — nighttime turn-down is operationally practical without combustion instability.

Stack height matters Night turn-down −3 to −6 dBA

05 · Monitoring Protocol

Fenceline Noise Monitoring Program

Continuous fenceline monitoring is best practice for any 24/7 generation asset adjacent to mixed-use or residential areas in Cleveland. While §605.10 does not mandate self-monitoring, Ohio EPA PTIO conditions for stationary combustion equipment routinely impose initial commissioning tests and complaint-triggered measurements. The FixOurStreets216 Project recommends the following permanent monitoring architecture.

01
Pre-Construction Ambient Baseline Survey
Minimum 7-day continuous ambient measurement at proposed fenceline stations before any equipment installation. Establishes daytime Leq and nighttime Leq for comparison post-startup. Measurement per ANSI S12.18 / ISO 1996-2.
PRE-PERMIT
02
Commissioning Performance Test
Full-load and part-load noise emission test at fenceline and at 50 m / 100 m intermediate points during commissioning. Octave-band measurement per ANSI S1.13. Silencer IL verification against design guarantee. Results submitted to Ohio EPA with PTIO startup notification.
STARTUP
03
Permanent Fenceline Monitoring Network
Install minimum 3 permanently-mounted Class 1 sound level meters at cardinal fenceline points (nearest receptor on each exposure face). Log Leq, Lmax, and L90 at 1-minute intervals. Integration with site SCADA enables automatic alert when Leq approaches 58 dBA (2 dB warning margin before 60 dBA target). Vendor: Svantek SV 307A or Norsonic Nor145 environmental noise monitors.
ONGOING
04
Annual Verification & Silencer Condition Inspection
Third-party acoustic survey annually per OSHA/ANSI S12.18 to verify continued compliance. Visual and boroscope inspection of exhaust silencer internals for mineral wool degradation, baffle erosion, or structural damage (high-temperature exhaust environments degrade absorptive media over time — plan for 7–10 year media replacement cycle).
ANNUAL
05
Complaint Response Protocol
Any complaint received from City of Cleveland Division of Environment & Sustainability or an adjacent property owner triggers a 24-hour attended measurement by a DPLE-certified acoustician. If Leq at fenceline exceeds 63 dBA daytime or 53 dBA nighttime (pre-enforcement margin), initiate corrective action plan within 72 hours per §605.10 compliance posture.
EVENT

07 · Key Notes & Cautions

Critical Design & Compliance Cautions

⚠
Multi-unit energy summation: Each additional turbine unit adds ~3 dBA to the combined source level (energy summation — not simple arithmetic). A 4-unit L30A campus reaches a combined source level ~6 dBA higher than a single unit. All propagation models must use the combined source strength, not per-unit values.
⚠
Low-frequency limits of barriers: Acoustic barriers are nearly ineffective at the 63–125 Hz octave bands that dominate turbine exhaust. Do not plan to meet compliance with barriers alone. Inline exhaust silencers with verified low-frequency IL performance are non-negotiable for Cleveland §605.10(b)(5) compliance.
⚠
Silencer media degradation at high temperature: The L30A exhaust exits at ~500 °C. Conventional fiberglass silencer media degrades rapidly above 450 °C. Specify high-temperature mineral wool (e.g., Superwool 607 or equivalent, rated to 650 °C) and confirm the baffle facing design prevents erosion of media into the exhaust stream. Plan for inspection at 5 years and media replacement at 8–10 years.
i
Hydrogen combustion and noise: The Kawasaki L30A DLE system supports up to 60% H₂ by volume in mixed combustion. Higher hydrogen content increases flame speed and combustion energy but the DLE design controls combustion noise within normal operational ranges. No additional acoustic treatment is required for the H₂ combustion mode specifically, but commissioning noise tests should be conducted at the planned maximum H₂ blend ratio to confirm.
i
Cleveland §605.10 is complaint-triggered: Unlike some jurisdictions, Cleveland does not conduct routine industrial noise inspections. Enforcement is primarily complaint-triggered. However, the Ohio EPA PTIO process provides the teeth for pre-permit compliance assurance — conditions in the PTIO can include specific dBA limits, monitoring requirements, and compliance demonstration obligations. Design to the 60 dBA FOS216 internal target, not just the 65 dBA code floor.
