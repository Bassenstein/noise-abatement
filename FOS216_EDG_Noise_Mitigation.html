Section 01

Regulatory Framework — City of Cleveland

Cleveland's noise enforcement operates on two parallel tracks: the general nuisance standard under the Codified Ordinances, and zoning district performance standards. Both must be satisfied simultaneously. The operative limit for industrial stationary equipment at the fence line of a hyperscale facility is derived from the combination of §605.10(b)(5), the zoning code industrial performance standards, and the Ohio background ambient benchmarks adopted by enforcement practice.

C.C.O. §605.10(b)(5) — Unnecessary Noise

Prohibits the unreasonable discharge into the open air of the exhaust of any stationary internal combustion engine. No facility may operate an EDG in a manner that causes objectionable noise at a property line, regardless of decibel level. This is a qualitative nuisance standard — it supplements, not substitutes for, the quantitative limits below.

C.C.O. §605.10(b)(8) — Construction / Equipment Nighttime Restriction

Operation of mechanical or electrically powered apparatus between 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. is restricted within 500 feet of places of residence. EDG weekly test runs scheduled during these hours at facilities within 500 ft of residential zones require Director of Public Safety exemption. Emergency operations are exempt under §605.10(c).

Cleveland Zoning Code — Industrial Performance Standards (I-1, I-2, I-3) · §353

All noise-generating operations in industrial districts must be muffled so as not to become objectionable due to intermittence, beat frequency, shrillness, or intensity as measured at any property line of the tract. The city's enforcement benchmark for stationary source compliance in industrial-to-non-residential contexts is 60 dBA at the fence line during daytime hours, consistent with Ohio general industrial practice and the EPA-derived community noise goals used by Cleveland's Department of Public Health.

Design Target: The FixOurStreets216 Project recommends designing to 55 dBA at the fence line as a design basis — 5 dB below the primary 60 dBA industrial limit — to provide engineering margin, account for atmospheric variation, cumulative EDG fleet stacking at multi-unit facilities, and preemptively satisfy the residential nighttime limit. Do not design to the code limit; design to 5 dB under it.

Section 02

EDG Source Noise Characteristics

A hyperscale data center typically deploys multiple EDG units per critical power bus. Each generator constitutes an independent broadband noise source. The unmitigated source levels are far above the 60 dBA fence line limit at any practical setback distance, making active acoustic design mandatory rather than optional.

EDG Capacity Range Typical Source Level (1m) Est. Level @ 50 ft Est. Level @ 100 ft Primary Noise Paths
750 kW – 1.25 MW (standard HV unit) 98–102 dBA 78–82 dBA 72–76 dBA Exhaust, cooling fan, engine block radiation
1.5 MW – 2.5 MW (large HV unit) 102–107 dBA 82–87 dBA 76–81 dBA Exhaust dominant; intake secondary
3 MW+ (paralleled or utility-scale) 107–112 dBA 87–92 dBA 81–86 dBA Exhaust + structural resonance at enclosure

Noise reduction required from a typical 1.25 MW unit at a 100 ft fence line: ~17–21 dB total system attenuation to achieve 55 dBA. At 50 ft: ~22–27 dB. This requires a layered mitigation system — no single measure achieves this alone.

Key insight — octave band analysis required: Diesel generators produce dominant energy in the 63 Hz and 125 Hz octave bands (fundamental firing frequency and harmonics). A-weighting partially discounts low-frequency energy, but low-frequency rumble is the primary cause of neighbor complaints at distance. Specify acoustic treatments with insertion loss data in each octave band from 63 Hz to 4 kHz, not just broadband dBA ratings.

Section 03

Mitigation Hierarchy — Layered Approach

EDG noise mitigation must be treated as a system, applied in priority order from the source outward. Each layer provides independent attenuation that compounds logarithmically — 15 dB from an enclosure plus 12 dB from a silencer yields a combined reduction of approximately 16–17 dB, not 27 dB. Plan accordingly.

Tier 1 — Source Control

Engine Specification & Procurement

Select generators with factory-rated sound attenuated enclosures. Specify maximum unattenuated source level in the procurement RFQ. Require ISO 3744/8528-10 test data with delivery.

3–8 dBReduction vs. open-deck baseline
  • Caterpillar Super Silent series standard as baseline
  • Cummins "Sound Attenuated" package — specify SA rating
  • Kohler factory enclosure — Level 1 or Level 2 acoustic
  • MTU OnSite genset with factory ATA silencer
Tier 1 — Source Control

Siting & Setback Optimization

Every doubling of distance reduces free-field sound by 6 dB. Maximize distance between EDG yard and property line. Locate units behind the main data hall mass — the building itself provides acoustic shielding.

6 dBPer doubling of distance (free field)
  • Target ≥ 150 ft setback from nearest fence line
  • Orient exhaust stacks away from nearest receptor
  • Leverage building mass as acoustic shadow
  • Avoid reflective surfaces (concrete walls) on receptor side
Tier 2 — Path Interruption

Acoustic Enclosure / Penthouse

A purpose-built acoustic penthouse or critical-grade generator enclosure provides the largest single block of attenuation in the system. Must include treated ventilation ducts — unlined openings nullify the enclosure.

15–25 dBSTC 40–55 construction target
  • 8" CMU + resilient channel + 5/8" drywall interior
  • Acoustically lined ventilation intake / exhaust plenums
  • Minimum STC 45 rated doors — Acoustics First or equal
  • Penetration seal with fire-rated acoustic caulk at all utility entries
Tier 2 — Path Interruption

Exhaust Silencer — Critical Grade

Exhaust noise is the highest energy source in a diesel generator, often 10–15 dB above engine body radiation. A properly sized critical-grade silencer is non-negotiable for compliance.

25–35 dBInsertion Loss — critical grade
  • Nelson Silencer — Gen-Set Critical or Super Critical
  • Burgess-Manning BKP Series — specify IL by octave band
  • Industrial Acoustics Company Gen-Critical
  • Size for rated exhaust flow at 100% load + 10% margin
Tier 2 — Path Interruption

Acoustic Barrier Wall

A solid barrier between the EDG yard and the fence line interrupts the direct sound path. Effectiveness is limited by diffraction over the top; wall must extend above the EDG exhaust stack to be effective.

8–15 dBInsertion loss — properly sized
  • Minimum 14 ft height above grade (taller than exhaust stack)
  • No gaps at base — caulk and seal to concrete pad
  • Mass ≥ 4 lb/ft² — concrete block or precast panel preferred
  • Extend 10 ft beyond EDG footprint each side (horizontal diffraction)
Tier 3 — Receiver Control

Vibration Isolation

Structure-borne vibration from the engine block transmits through the pad and building structure, re-radiating as airborne noise at remote locations. Anti-vibration mounts decouple the engine from the pad.

10–20 dBStructure-borne path reduction
  • Spring-type mounts — Mason Industries SLR or VMC Group equal
  • Flexible exhaust couplings — stainless bellows, 18"+ length
  • Flexible fuel, coolant, and electrical conduit connections
  • Inertia base — concrete pad atop spring array (seismic zone compatible)

Section 04

Acoustic Enclosure Design Specifications

The acoustic enclosure is the primary noise control measure at a hyperscale data center where close fence line setbacks are unavoidable. The following specifications represent the minimum construction standard for achieving the 55 dBA design target at the property line.

Critical principle — the "weakest path" rule: The overall insertion loss of an enclosure is limited by its weakest acoustic element. A wall with STC 55 is worthless if an unlined air intake duct provides a flanking path with only 10 dB attenuation. Treat every penetration, door, and ventilation opening as a potential failure point in the acoustic chain.

Section 05

Exhaust Silencer Specification

The exhaust system on a diesel generator is the single highest-noise-energy path in the system. A critical-grade or super-critical silencer is mandatory for compliance. Selection must be based on measured insertion loss data at rated engine load and exhaust temperature — not catalog broadband ratings.

Procurement warning: Most standard residential or "hospital grade" silencers provide 15–20 dB IL — insufficient for a hyperscale facility. Specify only "critical grade" (20–28 dB IL) or "super critical grade" (28–35 dB IL) models. Require manufacturer-certified octave-band insertion loss test data (63 Hz – 8 kHz) per ISO 11820 or ASTM E477 with each submittal. Reject any product whose IL data is broadband only.
Grade Broadband IL 63 Hz IL 125 Hz IL 250 Hz+ IL Application
Residential / Standard 12–18 dB 8–12 dB 10–15 dB 15–20 dB Not suitable for hyperscale
Industrial 18–23 dB 12–18 dB 18–22 dB 20–25 dB Minimum acceptable with full enclosure
Critical Grade 23–28 dB 18–23 dB 22–27 dB 25–30 dB RECOMMENDED MINIMUM
Super Critical 28–35 dB 23–30 dB 27–33 dB 30–38 dB Where setback < 100 ft or residential adjacent

Stack the silencer outside the enclosure when possible, exhausting vertically through the roof with the silencer body inside the enclosure. This keeps silencer radiated noise within the enclosure shell. Insulate the exhaust stack above the silencer with 2" mineral wool + 16 ga stainless jacket to prevent stack body radiation.

Section 06

Vibration Isolation Design

Structure-borne vibration is the invisible compliance failure mode. An EDG can pass airborne noise tests but still generate complaints from neighbors experiencing structure-borne rumble propagating through the building's foundation into the ground. Anti-vibration design is mandatory, not optional.

Anti-Vibration Mount Selection

  • Type: Spring-type mounts (deflection ≥ 1.5", preferably 2") — not rubber pads
  • Natural frequency: ≤ 3 Hz; engine fire frequency typically 10–25 Hz → isolation ratio > 10:1
  • Seismic snubbers: Required; set gap to 0.25" for seismic zone compliance
  • Manufacturers: Mason Industries SLR/SLRS, VMC Group GEN-mount, Vibro-Acoustics SSF series
  • Load sharing: All mounts at equal deflection under rated generator weight + 15% dynamic factor

Flexible Connections — All Systems

  • Exhaust: Stainless steel bellows connector, minimum 18" length, 2-axis flex
  • Fuel supply & return: Flexible hose with metal fittings, min. 24" free length
  • Coolant: Flexible hose, isolate from pipe hangers for 10 ft from unit
  • Electrical conduit: Flexible metal conduit minimum 36" from genset junction box
  • Jacket water piping: Spring hangers within 20 ft of unit on hard-piped systems

Inertia Base (When Required)

  • Weight: 1.5× to 2× the operating weight of the generator set
  • Concrete: 3,000 psi minimum; embedded steel channel for anchorage
  • Clearance: Minimum 2" air gap on all sides between inertia base and floor/walls
  • Housekeeping pad: Isolate from slab with 1" neoprene or spring perimeter isolation

Ground-Borne Vibration Limits

  • At fence line: < 0.01 in/sec PPV (velocity) per FTA Category 1 criterion
  • Measurement standard: ANSI S2.47 / ISO 2631 — use triaxial accelerometer at grade
  • Typical EDG unmitigated: 0.08–0.15 in/sec PPV at 50 ft
  • With proper spring mounts: 0.002–0.005 in/sec PPV at 50 ft
  • Compliance threshold: 0.012 in/sec (continuous) per ANSI S3.29

Section 07

Commissioning & Ongoing Monitoring Protocol

No acoustic design can be confirmed compliant without measurement under actual operating conditions. The following protocol establishes the minimum commissioning and ongoing monitoring requirements for EDG noise at a Cleveland hyperscale facility.

01

Design Phase

Acoustic Modeling — Pre-Construction

Engage a licensed acoustical engineer to perform a pre-construction noise model (SoundPlan, CadnaA, or equivalent) incorporating the EDG fleet layout, enclosure specifications, barrier design, and site topography. Model must include octave-band analysis and predict fence line levels under all EDG configurations (1 unit, partial fleet, full fleet). Submit model to the City of Cleveland Department of Building & Housing with permit application for major projects.

02

Construction Phase

Submittals & Shop Drawing Review

All acoustic materials — enclosure assemblies, silencers, mounts, flexible connectors — require acoustical engineer approval of shop drawings prior to fabrication. Specifically verify: silencer IL data (octave band), enclosure STC test results (per ASTM E90/E413), and spring mount load deflection curves. Substitutions require re-analysis of the acoustic model.

03

Commissioning — First Load Test

Baseline Fence Line Sound Survey

Conduct a Type 1 sound level survey (IEC 61672 Class 1 meter) at a minimum of 8 measurement positions around the full property perimeter during the first full-load EDG test run. Record 10-minute Leq at each position. Survey must occur with all EDGs at rated load, cooling fans at full speed, and all background HVAC operating. Positions must include the closest point on each fence line to the nearest EDG unit.

04

Ongoing Compliance

Annual Monitoring Program

Conduct annual fence line noise surveys during the scheduled annual full-load EDG test. Survey minimum 4 positions at closest fence line approaches. Document results and compare to baseline. Any position showing greater than 3 dB increase over baseline triggers an investigation and corrective action before the next test cycle. Maintain records for 7 years and make available to the City upon request.

05

Complaint Response

Neighbor Complaint Protocol

Upon receipt of any noise complaint related to EDG operation, conduct a same-day or next-business-day fence line measurement. If measurement confirms exceedance of 60 dBA daytime or 55 dBA nighttime, immediately suspend discretionary test runs until corrective action is implemented and re-verified. Document all complaint responses and measurements in a log maintained by the facility's Environmental Health & Safety officer.

Weekly test run scheduling: Program monthly or weekly EDG exercise tests between 9:00 AM and 4:00 PM on weekdays (not Saturdays or Sundays) to minimize community noise impact. Avoid scheduling full-fleet simultaneous testing — stagger unit tests across consecutive days to prevent cumulative multi-unit sound energy from exceeding compliance thresholds. Advance notification to adjacent facility operators is best practice.
