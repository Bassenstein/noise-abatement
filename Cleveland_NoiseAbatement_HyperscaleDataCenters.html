§ 01Overview

Scope, Purpose, and Cleveland Context

Scope & Applicability

This document establishes a technical framework for noise abatement and fence-line acoustic monitoring at hyperscale data centers proposed for siting within or adjacent to Cleveland's residential and mixed-use neighborhoods. It is intended for use by project developers, acoustic engineers, city permitting staff, and community benefit agreement negotiators.

A hyperscale data center, as defined here, is a facility of 100 MW or greater critical IT load, typically co-located with on-site power generation (gas turbine, combined cycle, linear generator, or fuel cell systems) and large mechanical cooling infrastructure. These facilities introduce noise sources of a scale, intensity, and operational continuity that standard commercial or industrial noise provisions were not designed to address.

ℹ
Applicability Threshold
This framework applies to any proposed Hyperscale Data Center facility with (a) aggregate installed generation capacity exceeding 50 MW, or (b) cooling tower aggregate fan motor capacity exceeding 2 MW, or (c) proposed siting within 1,000 feet of a residential zoning district boundary. Projects below these thresholds may still be subject to §551 but are not required to follow this full framework.

Cleveland Context

Cleveland's industrial corridors — the Industrial Valley, Opportunity Corridor, Slavic Village, and Collinwood manufacturing zones — are emerging targets for hyperscale data center development driven by available land, Great Lakes cooling water access, and the Midwest's growing power infrastructure. These sites frequently abut residential neighborhoods with pre-existing noise sensitivities and environmental justice considerations addressed under Cleveland Ordinance 2023-014.

Unlike traditional data centers (fan arrays, rooftop HVAC), hyperscale facilities often incorporate combustion turbines, combined heat and power systems, and large-tonnage absorption chilling — source types with sound power levels 20–40 dB above conventional data center noise floors. The standard §551 residential limit of 50 dB(A) at night is achievable but requires a disciplined, layered acoustic design program beginning at site selection.

⚡
Key Challenge: 24/7 Continuous Operation
Hyperscale data centers operate at or near full load continuously, including nighttime hours when §551 limits are most restrictive. A gas turbine at full load producing 118 dB(A) sound power at the exhaust stack must be attenuated to 50 dB(A) at a residential property line 300 meters away — requiring approximately 35–40 dB of combined attenuation from geometry, barriers, and active silencing systems.
§ 02Cleveland Codified Ordinance §551

Noise Control Limits, Measurement Protocol, and Penalty Provisions

Noise Limits by Receiving Zone

§551 establishes maximum permissible sound levels by the zoning classification of the receiving property — i.e., the zone adjacent to or beyond the data center's fence line, not the data center's own zoning. For hyperscale facilities, the most restrictive applicable limits are almost always driven by residential uses on adjacent parcels.

§551.02 Measurement Protocol

Cleveland CCO §551 — Key Measurement Requirements
§551.02(a)Sound level measurements shall be taken with a Type 1 or Type 2 sound level meter meeting ANSI S1.4 / IEC 61672-1 standards, A-weighted, slow response.
§551.02(b)Measurements shall be taken at or beyond the property line, at a height of 4–5 feet above grade, and no closer than 3 feet from any reflective surface (walls, fences, vehicles).
§551.02(c)A valid sample consists of the average of three consecutive 1-minute Leq readings under stable meteorological conditions.
§551.02(d)Meteorological validity conditions: wind speed less than 5 m/s (11 mph) at measurement height; no precipitation; background ambient at least 10 dB below the measured level to confirm source attribution.
§551.02(e)Atmospheric absorption correction per ISO 9613-1 is required for propagation paths exceeding 100 meters. Temperature and relative humidity must be recorded at each measurement event.
§551.05Enforcement authority rests with the Commissioner of Building and Housing. Continuous exceedances at a residential boundary constitute a public nuisance subject to abatement order.

Tonal and Impulsive Noise Penalties

§551 imposes a +5 dB(A) penalty on the measured level when the noise contains audible tonal components (discrete frequency prominences of 5 dB or more above the adjacent 1/3-octave bands) or impulsive characteristics. For hyperscale data centers, two source types routinely trigger these penalties if not properly designed:

⚠
Tonal Penalty — Linear Generator (LGen) Blade-Pass Frequency
Mainspring and similar linear reciprocating generator systems produce a strong tonal component at the piston operating frequency (typically 60 Hz and harmonics). At residential boundaries, this tonal content can trigger the +5 dB penalty, effectively reducing the available headroom from 10 dB to 5 dB. Acoustic enclosures with a minimum 10 dB(A) insertion loss are required at the LGen building envelope. Blade-pass frequency content must be measured at the fence line and confirmed below the tonal threshold before commissioning.
⚠
Tonal Penalty — Gas Turbine Blade-Pass / Combustion Tone
Gas turbines (GE Vernova 7HA series, Kawasaki H2 turbines) produce blade-pass frequency content in the 500–4,000 Hz range depending on rotor speed and stage count. Combustion dynamics can introduce low-frequency tonal content (50–200 Hz) that propagates over long distances. Exhaust silencer design must target tonal content, not just broadband A-weighted levels. Third-octave spectra measurement at fence line is required during commissioning to confirm no tonal penalties apply.

Perimeter Noise Barriers

Perimeter barriers serve as the final layer of attenuation, catching noise that has passed through or around the primary source-level mitigations. Barrier design must be coordinated with the acoustic model — a barrier that is too short, too narrow, or improperly located can provide as little as 3–5 dB insertion loss rather than the 10–15 dB achievable with a properly dimensioned wall.

⚠
Barrier Modeling Requirement
All perimeter barriers must be sized using a validated acoustic propagation model (ISO 9613-2, FHWA TNM, or equivalent). Barrier heights and lengths derived from rule-of-thumb tables are insufficient for the source complexity of a hyperscale campus. A full 3D model incorporating building shielding, ground absorption, and atmospheric effects is required as part of the air permit application and community benefit agreement technical annexes.
§ 05Fence Line Monitoring

Monitoring Point Placement and Operational Requirements

Monitor Point Placement Criteria

Fence-line monitoring points must be placed to capture the worst-case noise impact at each distinct receiving-zone boundary. A minimum of one point per distinct zoning boundary segment is required; additional points are required wherever a dominant noise source (GT exhaust axis, cooling tower cluster face) is within 150 meters of a residential boundary.

Relationship to Acoustic Mitigation Package
The monitoring program represents approximately 1–2% of a typical hyperscale acoustic mitigation package ($10–18M Class 4). It is the verification layer — it proves the mitigation is performing. Monitoring program costs should be included in the Community Benefits Agreement as a separate line item and maintained as a long-term facility operating expense, not a one-time capital cost.
§ 07Implementation Phasing

Acoustic Compliance Program — Phased Implementation

01
Pre-Construction · 12–18 Months Before First Fire
Baseline Survey, Acoustic Modeling, and Permit Submittals
Establish the pre-construction ambient noise environment at all six fence-line monitoring points. Commission the 3D acoustic propagation model and use it to size all mitigation systems. Complete §551 and Title V noise impact analyses for air permit application.
  • Pre-construction ambient survey — minimum 72-hour continuous at each residential boundary point
  • 3D acoustic model completed and peer-reviewed (ISO 9613-2 or FHWA TNM)
  • Mitigation system sizing finalized and incorporated into design documents
  • Title V noise impact analysis submitted to Ohio EPA
  • §551 compliance demonstration submitted to Cleveland Building & Housing
  • CBA technical annex noise provisions negotiated and executed
02
Construction Phase · 6–12 Months Before First Fire
Monitoring Infrastructure Installation and Equipment Procurement
Install permanent monitoring stations, meteorological equipment, and data infrastructure while construction is underway. Verify instrument placement against the acoustic model before concrete barriers are complete.
  • Procure and install 6× permanent outdoor SLM stations on fence line
  • Install meteorological stations and commission datalogger integration
  • Install vibration/accelerometer instrumentation on LGen foundations and GT area
  • Commission data backhaul to DCS and noise monitoring software platform
  • Verify SLM placement against model — adjust if shielding or ground reflections differ from model assumptions
  • Deliver 3× portable Type 1 SLMs to acoustic engineer team
03
Commissioning · First 30 Days of Operation
Commissioning Acoustic Survey and Tonal Assessment
The most critical acoustic milestone. All mitigation systems must be verified performing as designed during actual plant operation before commercial operation date (COD). This is the only opportunity to identify design shortfalls before the facility is occupied and neighbors have legal standing to complain.
  • First fire + load ramp: continuous monitoring active at all 6 points from first startup
  • 1/3-octave spectra captured at all residential boundary points during: startup, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% load
  • Tonal penalty assessment at LGen and GT boundary points — confirm no tonal prominences ≥ 5 dB above adjacent 1/3-oct bands
  • CT fan speed reduction verification after 22:00 — confirm residential boundary readings comply with nighttime limits at full cooling load
  • Mitigation shortfall identification and corrective action plan issued within 14 days of commissioning survey
  • 30-day operational baseline report submitted to CBA monitor and Cleveland APCD
04
Operations · Ongoing Annual Cycle
Continuous Monitoring, Annual Reporting, and Adaptive Management
Ongoing monitoring feeds both regulatory reporting and operational management. The DCS integration enables real-time noise-aware load control — the facility can reduce CT fan speed or defer LGen starts during sensitive nighttime hours without manual intervention.
  • Continuous monitoring at all 6 points; rolling 24h Leq logged and auto-compared to §551 limits
  • Exceedance events: automated alert within 5 minutes; root cause analysis and corrective action within 48 hours
  • Annual compliance report submitted to Cleveland APCD and CBA monitor — rolling 12-month Leq by point, all exceedance events, and corrective actions
  • Annual field calibration verification; A2LA lab certification for portable instruments
  • 5-year acoustic performance review — repeat commissioning-level survey to verify no degradation in mitigation system performance

City of Cleveland Review Requirements — Acoustic Submittals
Projects subject to this framework must submit the following to the Cleveland Department of Building and Housing and the Office of Sustainability prior to issuance of building permits: (1) Pre-construction ambient noise survey report; (2) Validated 3D acoustic propagation model with all source inputs and mitigation assumptions documented; (3) §551 compliance demonstration for each fence-line point and each receiving zone; (4) Mitigation design specifications including silencer insertion loss certifications and barrier design drawings; (5) Fence-line monitoring plan identifying point locations, instrumentation, and data management protocol; (6) Community Benefits Agreement noise annex incorporating the operational monitoring commitments. Post-construction: commissioning acoustic survey report within 60 days of first commercial operation date.
